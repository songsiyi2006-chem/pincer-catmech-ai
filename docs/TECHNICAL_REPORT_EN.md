# Pincer-CatMech-AI: Statistical Thermochemistry and Three-Dimensional Steric Descriptors

Technical report, core pipeline initialization; 13 September 2026.

## Abstract

Pincer-catalyzed borrowing-hydrogen transformations couple alcohol dehydrogenation, bond construction, and hydrogen return. Mechanistic comparison therefore requires consistent free energies for species with different coordination, protonation, and molecularity. This implementation provides an auditable postprocessing core: Gaussian/ORCA vibrational and thermochemical parsing, Grimme entropy interpolation, pressure-to-concentration standard-state conversion, exact donor–metal–donor angles, and Monte Carlo estimates of ligand buried volume. It does not execute electronic-structure calculations, identify a catalytic pathway, train a predictive model, or establish experimental catalyst performance. The statistical model is explicit, the distinction between total and vibrational entropy is preserved, and uncertainty in numerical volume integration is reported independently of uncertainty in the chemical model.

## 1. Chemical motivation and attribution

In borrowing-hydrogen N-alkylation, an alcohol supplies a carbonyl intermediate and transferable hydrogen equivalents; condensation with an amine and subsequent reduction can produce the alkylated amine. A defined Mn-PNP system demonstrates experimental feasibility for this reaction class, but its results are literature evidence, not results generated by this repository. [Elangovan et al., Nature Communications 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5059641/).

Metal–ligand cooperation (MLC) describes participation of ligand functionality in reactivity, including proton transfer and changes in ligand bonding. An outer-sphere hydrogen-transfer step need not contain a substrate–metal coordination bond. An inner-sphere route can instead contain substrate coordination and hydride migration. These categories are hypotheses to evaluate for a particular catalyst, substrate, spin state, and solvent; a PNP ligand or N–H bond does not by itself establish bifunctional catalysis.

Cheng Hou's coauthored 2015 Co-PNP study provides a particularly relevant counterexample: its calculations favored an inner-sphere non-bifunctional mechanism and connected the preference to metal geometry and electronic configuration. The paper lists Sun Yat-sen University affiliations and must be identified as earlier work by Hou and collaborators, rather than as a Guangxi Normal University group publication. [Hou et al., Dalton Transactions 2015, DOI 10.1039/C5DT02163D](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2015/dt/c5dt02163d/unauth).

Representative Guangxi Normal University work with Hou as corresponding author includes the 2022 study of borrowing-hydrogen N-alkylation by a Ru complex containing two proton-responsive sites. It distinguishes ligand distortion from productive N–H-mediated noncovalent assistance. This motivates preserving competing mechanistic assignments rather than equating every ligand N–H group with proton-transfer participation. [Mei et al., Dalton Transactions 2022, DOI 10.1039/D2DT02597C](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2022/dt/d2dt02597c).

The group's 2025 study combines DFT and machine learning for borrowing-hydrogen reactions with N-heterocyclic ligands, and its 2026 Ru(II) transfer-hydrogenation study also integrates DFT with machine learning. These papers support the relevance of traceable energetic and structural descriptors. Their particular metal/ligand sets and published prediction statistics are not transferable validation of this package. [Mo and Hou, 2025, DOI 10.1039/D5QO01139F](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2025/qo/d5qo01139f); [Zhou and Hou, 2026, DOI 10.1039/D6QO00178E](https://pubs.rsc.org/de/content/articlelanding/2026/qo/d6qo00178e).

The repository is an independently initialized software project informed by these research questions. It claims neither authorship, participation, endorsement, nor adoption by Cheng Hou's group. Its APIs accept Ru/Mn/Co/Fe-containing structures, but that interoperability does not establish comparable accuracy across metals, oxidation states, or spin manifolds.

## 2. Statistical-thermodynamic foundation

### 2.1 Units and independent degrees of freedom

Let the reported positive wavenumber be $\tilde\nu_i$ in $\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$. The ordinary frequency in hertz and dimensionless excitation energy are

$$\nu_i=c(100\tilde\nu_i),\qquad x_i=\frac{h\nu_i}{k_{\mathrm B}T}=\frac{100hc\tilde\nu_i}{k_{\mathrm B}T}.$$

Here $c$ is in $\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, $h$ in $\mathrm{J\,s}$, $k_{\mathrm B}$ in $\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$, and $T>0$ in kelvin. Substitution of a wavenumber directly into $h\nu$ without the speed-of-light conversion is dimensionally incorrect. $R=N_{\mathrm A}k_{\mathrm B}$ produces molar rather than single-molecule entropy.

Within the separable RRHO approximation, the molecular partition function factorizes as $q=q_{\rm el}q_{\rm trans}q_{\rm rot}\prod_iq_i$. Molecular rotation and translation remain separate from the low-frequency internal-rotor surrogate. The Gaussian thermochemistry documentation specifies the conventional contribution accounting used to interpret its output. [Gaussian thermochemistry reference](https://gaussian.com/wp-content/uploads/dl/thermo.pdf).

### 2.2 Harmonic oscillator partition function and entropy

For energies $\epsilon_n=h\nu_i(n+\tfrac12)$ and $\beta=(k_{\mathrm B}T)^{-1}$, summing the geometric series yields

$$q_i=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta h\nu_i(n+1/2)}=\frac{e^{-x_i/2}}{1-e^{-x_i}},\qquad\ln q_i=-\frac{x_i}{2}-\ln(1-e^{-x_i}).$$

The molar internal energy is

$$U_i=-N_{\mathrm A}\frac{\partial\ln q_i}{\partial\beta}=N_{\mathrm A}h\nu_i\left(\frac12+\frac1{e^{x_i}-1}\right).$$

Using $S_i=R[\ln q_i+T(\partial\ln q_i/\partial T)]$ gives cancellation of the explicit zero-point contribution:

$$S_i^{\rm HO}=R\left[\frac{x_i}{e^{x_i}-1}-\ln(1-e^{-x_i})\right].$$

Thus $\mathrm{ZPVE}=\tfrac12N_{\mathrm A}\sum_i h\nu_i$ contributes to energy, while the harmonic entropy depends on excitation probabilities. As $x_i\rightarrow0^+$,

$$S_i^{\rm HO}=R[1-\ln x_i+O(x_i^2)],$$

which diverges logarithmically. Soft torsions and weak collective motions are consequently sensitive to the harmonic approximation even when their electronic energies are well converged. As $x_i\rightarrow\infty$, the entropy vanishes.

The implementation evaluates these expressions with cancellation-resistant exponential/logarithmic forms and asymptotic handling. Numerical stabilization preserves the selected model; it does not turn a torsion into a measured normal mode.

### 2.3 Free-rotor surrogate and reduced inertia

Grimme's low-frequency treatment replaces the entropy of sufficiently soft modes by a rotor-like contribution. The original method and subsequent explicit derivation establish the reduced-inertia convention used here. [Grimme, 2012, DOI 10.1002/chem.201200497](https://doi.org/10.1002/chem.201200497); [explicit equations for the reduced inertia](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6467729/).

For a classical one-dimensional rotor with inertia $\mu_i'$, the momentum integral is

$$q_i^{\rm FR}=\frac1h\int_0^{2\pi}d\phi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left[-\frac{p_\phi^2}{2\mu_i'k_{\mathrm B}T}\right]dp_\phi=\left(\frac{8\pi^3\mu_i'k_{\mathrm B}T}{h^2}\right)^{1/2}.$$

The effective single-mode rotor convention uses unit internal symmetry number. Since $\ln q_i^{\rm FR}$ contains $\tfrac12\ln T$, its entropy is

$$S_i^{\rm FR}=R\left[\frac12+\ln\left(\frac{8\pi^3\mu_i'k_{\mathrm B}T}{h^2}\right)^{1/2}\right].$$

The wavenumber-derived inertia and its reduction are

$$\mu_i=\frac{h}{8\pi^2\nu_i},\qquad\mu_i'=\frac{\mu_i B_{\rm av}}{\mu_i+B_{\rm av}},\qquad B_{\rm av}=10^{-44}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2}.$$

The directive's displayed $h/(8\pi^2\nu_i)$ is the **unreduced** inertia. The default `rotor_inertia="grimme"` applies the reduction above, so that $\mu_i'\to B_{\rm av}$ as $\nu_i\to0^+$. The resulting low-frequency rotor entropy is finite. Explicit `rotor_inertia="uncapped"` reproduces the literal displayed approximation and is recorded as a different model; it must not be described as the full reduced-inertia Grimme treatment.

### 2.4 Smooth interpolation and physical interpretation

With the default crossover $\tilde\nu_0=100\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ and exponent four,

$$w_i=\frac1{1+(\tilde\nu_0/\tilde\nu_i)^4},\qquad S_{\rm vib}^{q}=\sum_i\left[w_iS_i^{\rm HO}+(1-w_i)S_i^{\rm FR}\right].$$

The limits are rotor-like at low frequency and harmonic at high frequency. At the crossover, $w_i=1/2$. The finite reduced-inertia rotor entropy and $w_i\sim\tilde\nu_i^4$ ensure a finite $\tilde\nu_i\to0^+$ limit of the interpolated expression. The crossover and $B_{\rm av}$ are modeling parameters, not constants deduced from the electronic Hamiltonian.

Entropy interpolation is a statistically motivated approximation built from oscillator and rotor limits. It is not an exact quantum treatment of coupled hindered rotors, solvent friction, or conformer interconversion. In particular, $S_{\rm vib}^{q}<S_{\rm vib}^{\rm HO}$ is a useful fixture check for the specified low-frequency-dominated spectrum, not a universal inequality for every spectrum, temperature, and parameter choice.

### 2.5 Total entropy and prevention of double counting

The input `S_harmonic` is the **total** RRHO molar entropy, while $S_{\rm vib}^{\rm HO}$ above contains only the included positive internal modes. The corrected total is

$$S_{\rm total}^{q}=S_{\rm total}^{\rm RRHO}-S_{\rm vib}^{\rm HO}+S_{\rm vib}^{q}.$$

This preserves the input electronic, translational, and molecular-rotational contributions. The pipeline makes an **entropy-only replacement** and retains the supplied RRHO enthalpy:

$$G_{p^\circ}^{q}=H_{\rm RRHO}(T)-T S_{\rm total}^{q}=G_{p^\circ}^{\rm RRHO}+T(S_{\rm vib}^{\rm HO}-S_{\rm vib}^{q}).$$

In the API, energies are $\mathrm{kcal\,mol^{-1}}$ and entropies are $\mathrm{cal\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$, so the implemented product is $TS/1000$. `H_298` denotes total enthalpy including electronic energy, ZPVE, and thermal terms; neither `E_elec` nor `ZPVE` is added to it again. The field name is retained for the requested schema; `temperature` records the actual state and must accompany the field at temperatures other than 298.15 K.

## 3. Standard state: 1 atm to 1 mol L⁻¹

At fixed temperature the ideal-gas chemical potential satisfies $\mu(p)=\mu(p^\circ)+RT\ln(p/p^\circ)$. The ideal-gas concentration corresponding to $p^\circ$ is $C_g^\circ=p^\circ/(RT)$. Referencing the same ideal chemical potential to concentration $C^\circ$ therefore gives

$$\Delta G_{\rm conc}=RT\ln\left(\frac{C^\circ}{C_g^\circ}\right)=RT\ln\left(\frac{C^\circ RT}{p^\circ}\right).$$

For SI evaluation, $C^\circ=1000\ \mathrm{mol\,m^{-3}}$, $p^\circ=101325\ \mathrm{Pa}$, and $T=298.15\ \mathrm K$. The dimensionless ratio is $24.4654036970$ and the correction is $+1.8943284455\ \mathrm{kcal\,mol^{-1}}$, which rounds to the directive's $+1.894$. The code evaluates the state-dependent expression rather than hard-coding a rounded 298.15 K value.

The reported standard-state-adjusted quantity is

$$G_{1\mathrm M}^{q}=H_{\rm RRHO}(T)-\frac{T S_{\rm total}^{q}}{1000}+\Delta G_{\rm conc}.$$

For reaction stoichiometric coefficients $\zeta_j$ positive for products and negative for reactants,

$$\Delta_rG_{1\mathrm M}^{q}=\sum_j\zeta_jG_{p^\circ,j}^{q}+\Delta n\,\Delta G_{\rm conc},\qquad\Delta n=\sum_j\zeta_j.$$

For $A+B\to AB$, $\Delta n=-1$, so the reaction shift is negative even though every individual species receives a positive correction. A bimolecular activation free energy referenced to $A+B$ has the same stoichiometric issue. Cancellation occurs for equal molecularity. Actual nonstandard activities require the separate $RT\ln Q$ term.

A 1 M reference does **not** create solvent stabilization. Gas-phase electronic energies remain gas-phase energies after this shift. A solution free energy additionally needs a documented solvation model, consistent standard states, and any required explicit solvent/speciation treatment. `G_298_qRRHO_sol` is the requested compatibility field name for the adjusted value; it is not proof that solvation was calculated. If an external solvation contribution already includes a standard-state conversion, adding the same conversion again is incorrect.

## 4. Parsing, typed data, and failure boundaries

`src/pincer_catmech/kinetics/free_energy.py` separates frequency parsing, scalar thermochemistry extraction, and physical correction. `calculate_thermochemistry` accepts a spectrum plus `E_elec`, `ZPVE`, `H_298`, and `S_harmonic`, with explicit temperature, pressure, concentration, crossover, rotor convention, and transition-state status. The validated Pydantic dataclass `ThermochemistryResult` retains the required energetic/entropic fields and interpretation metadata.

Gaussian `Frequencies --` records are distinct from vibrational intensities and displacement vectors. ORCA vibrational sections contain indexed modes and may include translational/rotational zero modes. Reading must remain within the relevant frequency and thermochemistry block rather than accepting every number in an output file. A printed normal termination establishes program completion only; the user still must verify electronic convergence and stationary-point character.

ORCA thermochemistry can already use qRRHO and prints entropy components as **$T S$ energy terms**. The pipeline reconstructs a harmonic total from the printed electronic, translational, and rotational terms plus its own harmonic vibrational entropy, then applies its chosen interpolation once. It does not subtract a harmonic vibrational entropy from an already-damped total. ORCA's documented use of molecularly averaged inertia also differs from fixed $B_{\rm av}$, so canonical reprocessing need not numerically reproduce ORCA's original qRRHO value. [ORCA thermochemistry manual](https://orca-manual.mpi-muelheim.mpg.de/contents/structurereactivity/thermochemistry.html).

Zero/imaginary modes, absent thermochemical fields, nonfinite input, nonpositive temperature/pressure/concentration, and ambiguous job boundaries require explicit handling. A minimum must have no imaginary modes; `transition_state=True` requires exactly one, which is excluded from the bound-mode partition sum. Manual zero-mode exclusion requires `exclude_zero_modes=True`; the ORCA parser only recognizes five or six consecutive initial zero modes as projected external modes. Replacing an imaginary frequency by its absolute value would manufacture a bound vibration and can conceal a failed optimization.

`parse_thermochemistry` selects a completed job with `job_index` and checks that its full state matches any temperature override. A failed or unfinished final job is not silently replaced by an earlier successful result; selecting an earlier completed job requires an explicit index. Missing values are never borrowed across jobs.

“Recovery” in this pipeline means deterministic parsing and explicit diagnostics for supported cases. It does not mean inventing missing energies, guessing a spin state, silently converting a saddle into a minimum, or filling unknown atom radii. Manual total entropy and enthalpy inputs must refer to the same geometry, temperature, pressure, and frequency model. Changing only an entropy temperature while retaining an unrelated enthalpy is not a thermodynamically consistent recalculation.

## 5. Steric and geometric definitions

### 5.1 Exact donor–metal–donor angle

For zero-based atom indices identifying a metal $M$ and two donors, define $\mathbf a=\mathbf r_{L_1}-\mathbf r_M$ and $\mathbf b=\mathbf r_{L_2}-\mathbf r_M$. Then

$$\theta=\frac{180}{\pi}\arccos\left[\operatorname{clip}\left(\frac{\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b}{\|\mathbf a\|\|\mathbf b\|},-1,1\right)\right].$$

Clipping only repairs roundoff at the inverse-cosine boundary. A zero-length coordination vector is invalid and cannot be repaired by clipping. The angle is invariant to translation and rigid rotation. Orthogonal vectors give $90^\circ$; opposite vectors give $180^\circ$. For a pincer, terminal P–M–P and adjacent P–M–N angles are different descriptors, so donor identities must be recorded rather than inferred from a single “bite angle” label.

`bite_angle` takes the structure, metal index, and two donor indices. It accepts ASE `Atoms`, a single-frame XYZ path, or a Cartesian array accompanied by element symbols. All coordinates are in ångström; periodic structures are rejected. Coordinates alone do not define an atom-specific radius model.

### 5.2 Buried volume as a union of atomic balls

Let $B_R(M)$ be the probe sphere centered on the metal and $\mathcal L$ the explicitly selected ligand atoms. With scaled atomic radii $a_j=s r_j^{\rm Bondi}$,

$$f=\frac{\operatorname{Vol}\left[B_R(M)\cap\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal L}B_{a_j}(\mathbf r_j)\right]}{4\pi R^3/3},\qquad\%V_{\rm bur}=100f.$$

The union counts overlapping atoms once. Defaults are $R=3.5\ \text{Å}$ and $s=1.17$, with hydrogens excluded unless explicitly requested. These parameters follow the documented SambVca convention; they are empirical descriptor settings, not a unique physical boundary of the molecule. [SambVca parameter documentation](https://www.aocdweb.com/OMtools/sambvca2.1/help/help.html).

The metal is a reference center, not an occluding ligand atom. `ligand_indices` prevents substrates, counterions, solvent molecules, and other ligands from silently changing the intended descriptor. Unsupported element radii require explicit overrides with recorded provenance. Neither a covalent-radius table nor an unrelated elemental extension is silently called a Bondi table.

### 5.3 Uniform sampling and statistical uncertainty

Draw independent $u,v,w\sim U(0,1)$ and set $r=Rw^{1/3}$, $z=2u-1$, $\phi=2\pi v$. A sphere-uniform point is

$$\mathbf X=\mathbf r_M+r\left(\sqrt{1-z^2}\cos\phi,\sqrt{1-z^2}\sin\phi,z\right).$$

The radial exponent follows from $P(r'\le r)=r^3/R^3$. Sampling radius uniformly would overweight the center. For each sample, $I_k=1$ if at least one selected atomic ball contains $\mathbf X_k$, otherwise $I_k=0$. Then

$$\hat f=\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^NI_k,\qquad\widehat{\%V}_{\rm bur}=100\hat f,\qquad\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}=100\sqrt{\frac{\hat f(1-\hat f)}N}.$$

For normal quantile $z_*=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)$, the Wilson interval is $100(c_W-h_W,c_W+h_W)$, where

$$c_W=\frac{\hat f+z_*^2/(2N)}{1+z_*^2/N},\qquad h_W=\frac{z_*\sqrt{\hat f(1-\hat f)/N+z_*^2/(4N^2)}}{1+z_*^2/N}.$$

Unlike a naive Wald interval, Wilson limits remain useful when the observed hit fraction is zero or one. This interval describes binomial sampling uncertainty conditional on one fixed geometry and radius convention. It does not include uncertainty from DFT, conformers, protonation states, ligand selection, or van der Waals radii. At $N=100000$, the worst-case normal standard error is about $0.1581$ percentage points; this is an analytical sampling bound, not a measured benchmark result.

`buried_volume` returns `BuriedVolumeResult`, including `percent_buried_volume` and `confidence_interval_percent`. The sample count, random seed, probe radius, scaling, hydrogen policy, and ligand selection belong with the descriptor. A fixed seed makes the calculation reproducible within the documented implementation; it does not remove sampling error.

## 6. Algorithmic complexity and the Voronoi comparison

For $N$ samples and $M$ selected ligand atoms, direct membership evaluation requires $O(NM)$ distance work. This implementation accumulates atomwise within sample chunks, using $O(B+M)$ working storage for chunk size $B$. A broadcast alternative would use $O(BM)$; the implemented method avoids a full $N\times M\times3$ allocation. Parsing is linear in output length, frequency correction is $O(F)$ for $F$ modes, and an angle calculation is $O(1)$ after input access.

A three-dimensional Voronoi diagram partitions nearest-site space; it does not directly give the clipped union of unequal atomic balls. A valid exact alternative needs appropriate weighted geometry, intersection with the probe sphere, and curved-boundary volume integration. Qhull explicitly does not supply weighted Voronoi diagrams or Voronoi volumes as a general facility. [Qhull capabilities and limitations](https://www.qhull.org/).

Three-dimensional Delaunay/Voronoi combinatorial size can be quadratic in $M$, although many ordinary point distributions yield much smaller diagrams. Thus a blanket claim that analytical Voronoi evaluation is always $O(M\log M)$ is unjustified. Precision, degeneracies, weighting, and spherical clipping affect the actual implementation. [CGAL 3D triangulation complexity](https://doc.cgal.org/Manual/latest/doc_html/cgal_manual/Triangulation_3/Chapter_main.html).

Monte Carlo offers simple union semantics and a directly interpretable sampling interval, at the cost of $N^{-1/2}$ convergence. Halving the stochastic error usually requires four times as many samples. This report establishes complexity and error scaling; it does not claim a measured speed advantage over an unimplemented analytical method.

## 7. Verification design and interpretation of benchmark data

`tests/test_pipeline.py` exercises the integrated thermodynamic and geometric APIs with explicitly synthetic Ru-PNP-themed spectra containing 25.4, 68.2, and 120.5 $\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ modes and higher-frequency stretches. These selected numbers are software fixtures. They are not an optimized Ru-PNP Hessian, a complete experimental spectrum, or a mechanistic energy profile.

The entropy check compares harmonic and interpolated totals for the chosen low-frequency-dominated fixture. Additional checks target dimensional conversion, harmonic/free-rotor limits, finite reduced inertia, total-entropy substitution, standard-state sign and temperature dependence, typed output, parser block boundaries, and deliberate rejection of invalid inputs. Numerical identities and independently evaluated scalar expressions provide stronger software evidence than reproducing the implementation expression in a test without an independent expectation.

Analytical vector geometries provide exact angle references. For volume, an isolated atomic sphere entirely inside the probe provides the reference fraction $(a/R)^3$; disjoint and fully enclosing configurations check union boundaries. Fixed-seed reproducibility and Wilson interval properties test numerical behavior. Such results validate geometry integration conditional on the specified inputs, not a relationship between buried volume and observed catalytic yield.

## 8. Reproducible operation and scientific use

The deployment script creates or reuses a local virtual environment, installs declared dependencies, and runs `pytest tests/ -v` before staging, committing, and pushing the authorized repository changes to `origin main`. Failure of a required test stops the deployment sequence. The script is a software gate; it cannot establish whether a proposed catalytic mechanism is chemically correct.

For a research dataset, retain source files and hashes, software version, functional/basis/ECP, dispersion and solvent settings, charge/multiplicity, convergence status, geometry identity, frequency scaling, retained/omitted modes, temperature, pressure, concentration, qRRHO parameters, and atom-index mappings. Compare species only after harmonizing those definitions. Keep failed calculations visible as failed records rather than assigning fabricated energies.

A later predictive study can use these descriptors with independently established targets and scaffold-aware validation splits. Reaction free energies, activation free energies, rates, and yields are distinct quantities. Conformational populations require a consistent ensemble free energy, and rate prediction additionally requires an elementary-step network, concentration dependence, and justified kinetic assumptions. This core supplies documented inputs for that work; it does not report an AI-discovered catalyst or a validated borrowing-hydrogen mechanism.
